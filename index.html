What is an electric vehicle?

An EV swaps the fuel tank and combustion engine for a battery and an electric motor. Electricity in, motion out, with far fewer moving parts in between.

The simple idea behind the machine

A battery electric vehicle (BEV) stores energy in a rechargeable pack that you charge from the grid, at home or from solar panels. That stored electricity is the only source of power for the wheels. Low-speed electric vehicles also exist for limited roads, but "EV" usually means a highway-capable car.

  • No tailpipe, so no exhaust in city air
  • Motors turn most of their energy into motion
  • Can run on solar, wind and other clean power

Battery pack

Stores the energy. Its size sets range and weight.

Electric motor

Converts electricity into rotation that drives the wheels.

Controller

Manages how much power flows when you press the pedal.

Onboard charger

Turns grid AC power into DC for the battery.

Types of EV

Battery Electric Vehicle (BEV)

Runs entirely on electricity from a rechargeable battery. Zero tailpipe emissions and the lowest running cost, but it needs charging infrastructure.

Fully electric

Plug-in Hybrid (PHEV)

Pairs a petrol engine with a larger battery you can plug in. Drive short trips on electricity, and use the engine for long journeys.

Electric + Petrol

Hybrid Electric Vehicle (HEV)

Uses a petrol engine and an electric motor together to save fuel. The battery recharges through regenerative braking, so you never plug it in.

Self-Charging

Fuel Cell Vehicle (FCEV)

Makes electricity onboard from hydrogen. The only emission is water vapour, and refuelling takes minutes, though hydrogen stations are rare.

Hydrogen

EVs and Sustainable Transportation

Cleaner cities

Removing exhaust cuts local smog and noise, improving public health where people live and work.

Renewable power

EVs run on whatever the grid provides, so they get greener as solar and wind grow.

Vehicle-to-grid

Parked EVs can store surplus clean energy and return it at peak hours, balancing the grid.

Battery Circularity

Used batteries can power home storage, then be recycled to recover lithium, nickel and cobalt.

Energy Efficiency

Electric drivetrains convert over 85% of electrical energy into motion, wasting far less power than combustion engines.

Decarbonized Transit

Electrifying public buses, rail, and commercial fleets scales emission reductions across entire urban transit networks.

Lower Lifetime Footprint

Over their full lifecycle, EVs produce significantly fewer net greenhouse gas emissions than petrol or diesel vehicles.

Reduced Fossil Dependence

Transitioning away from petroleum eliminates oil drilling impacts, transport spills, and refinery pollution.
