Basic Info about EVs

Electric cars operate on a simple principle: instead of using gas to power an internal combustion engine, they use electricity to power an electric motor. The electric motor turns the wheels, which propels the car. Sounds simple, doesn’t it? Yet the world of electric cars extends far beyond this basic principle. The term "electric car" typically refers specifically to battery electric vehicles (BEVs) or all-electric cars, a type of electric vehicle (EV) that has an onboard rechargeable battery pack that can be plugged in and charged from the electric grid, or at home with solar panels, and the electricity stored on the vehicle is the only energy source that provides propulsion for the wheels. The term generally refers to highway-capable automobiles, but there are also low-speed electric vehicles with limitations in terms of weight, power, and maximum speed that are allowed to travel on certain public roads.

Types of EV

Battery Electric Vehicle

Runs entirely on electricity stored in rechargeable batteries. BEVs produce zero tailpipe emissions and must be charged using an external power source. They are ideal for environmentally friendly and energy-efficient transportation.

Battery Electric Vehicle

Runs entirely on electricity stored in rechargeable batteries. BEVs produce zero tailpipe emissions and must be charged using an external power source. They are ideal for environmentally friendly and energy-efficient transportation.

Hybrid Electric Vehicle

Uses both a petrol engine and an electric motor to improve fuel efficiency. Unlike PHEVs, HEVs do not require external charging because the battery is recharged through regenerative braking while driving.

Fuel Cell Electric Vehicle

Generates electricity from hydrogen fuel cells instead of rechargeable batteries. FCEVs produce only water vapour as emissions and can be refuelled quickly, making them a promising option for clean and sustainable transportation.
